# Veloria — Player's Guide

You are King Adrian IV, the new ruler of an imaginary kingdom with modern institutions. Your goal is not to govern perfectly; it is to **stay on the throne for 104 weeks**. The army, intelligence service, media, oligarchs, courts, opposition, and your own family are power centres you must keep in balance.

> Every solution creates a new problem. The general who puts down a protest may one day stage a coup.

## Installation

The current release is **1.5.0**. The game is available for Windows, Linux, and the web.

| Platform | How to launch |
|---|---|
| Windows | Double-click `Veloria.exe`. No installation is required. |
| Linux | Make `Veloria.x86_64` executable (`chmod +x`) and run it. |
| Web | Serve the web package over HTTP and open `index.html`. A phone browser switches to the mobile layout automatically. |

The game opens in Turkish by default. Change the language in Settings; your choice is saved for the next launch.

## The desk

The desk is where each week begins. Click an object to open its screen:

| Object | Opens |
|---|---|
| Sealed letters | Agenda: decisions and reports |
| Newspaper | Media Centre |
| Telephone | People and actions |
| Reign diary | Your campaign record |
| Secret files | Secrets and the Promise Ledger |
| Dynasty portrait | Royal Archive and achievements |
| Capital map | Institutions |

Red badges mark things that need attention. Return to the desk with the **Desk** button in the top bar. In Settings, you can turn off **Start each week at the desk**.

**Ticker:** At the bottom of the screen, beside End Week, a single line summarizes the week: remaining actions, pending decisions (those expiring this week turn red), unread media, due promises, and spreading secrets. Click an item to jump to the relevant screen.

## The Capital

The map has seven institutions. Select one to see its leader, their report for the week, and institution-specific information—for example, files leading back to the Palace at the Court, or broken promises in the Assembly.

## Media Centre

- **The Veloria Herald:** Sarne's printed newspaper. It paints everything in a flattering light and denies Palace scandals.
- **The Free Press:** Leyla's independent paper. It is closer to the truth and publishes rumours from behind the scenes. Silence Leyla and it will close.
- **Halden TV:** Live broadcasts and a scrolling news ticker on a cathode-ray television.

If two papers tell different stories about the same week, the gap may show you what was left unsaid.

## The interface

- **Agenda:** Pending decisions (illustrated cards) and weekly reports. On desktop, the **THIS WEEK** ledger below the decisions records your actions and their consequences in order, even after a notification disappears. **SINCE LAST WEEK** summarizes changes in what you can observe: whose report worsened, who gained power, whose disposition hardened, which secret spread, which promise was kept or called in, and which decision resolved itself. The small arrow beside a report shows whether its source sounds more optimistic (green) or more pessimistic (red) than last week. It reflects what the source says, not necessarily the truth.
- **Action preview:** Hover over an action to see its cost (actions or wealth), the trace it leaves, and its estimated risk beneath the Actions panel. Warnings depend on the selected person—for example, you cannot blackmail someone whose weakness you have not uncovered.
- **Power centres:** Portraits of the people in your court. Select one to see what you know about them.
- **Orders and actions:** Actions you can take with the selected person.
- **Files:** Secrets, evidence, and the **Promise Ledger**.
- **Diary:** Your campaign record.
- **Top bar:** Week, remaining **actions** (gold diamonds), and **personal wealth**.

On mobile, people and actions are grouped under a separate **Palace** tab.

Early in the game, a **What should I do this week?** guide appears above the Agenda: 1. Make your decision → 2. Read the reports → 3. Take action with someone → 4. End the week. The next step is highlighted in gold; click a step to go there. Turn these tips off in Settings.

Reports appear below the power-centre portraits on desktop. Click a report to select its source. The coloured strip shows the tone of the report (green: optimistic, amber: uneasy, red: pessimistic), not the underlying truth.

## Basic rules

1. **Actions:** You have a limited number each week: 5 on Easy, 4 on Normal and Hard. Decisions, orders and operations use actions.
2. **No numbers:** You cannot see anyone's loyalty, the army's strength or public anger as a number. You get reports, and anyone can lie.
3. **Fear kills the truth:** The more people fear you, the less likely they are to bring bad news. If reports start sounding alike, no one is telling you the truth.
4. **Postponing is a decision:** Leave a decision card unanswered for two weeks and it resolves on its own, often in the worst possible way. Cards near their deadline flash red.
5. **The Council whispers:** Two advisers weigh in on each decision (for example, “I recommend II: it eases the treasury”). Their reasons are true but selective; they leave out costs that do not suit them. **HAS AN INTEREST** means one option directly affects that adviser. Advisers are more accurate in their area of expertise. Watch someone whose advice keeps clashing with the others. A frightened Council starts saying, “As you wish.”
6. **A coup does not come overnight:** A plot leaves **signs** first (shown as “SIGN: …” in the ticker). A person's card may label them **SUSPICIOUS** or **DANGEROUS**; the hint beside **Disposition** lists recent signs. Some signs are rumours. A reassured plotter may back down. When a coup reaches your door, a final **crisis card** appears: rally loyal troops, make a concession, or address the people. Its text describes the guards and the streets. If you do not decide that week, the Palace falls. A second concession may not be believed.
7. **The streets and the courts can bring you down:** Suppressing a crowd works, but stores up anger; each crackdown is less effective, and anger feeds unrest. The action preview warns you. If the squares fill, the **The Streets Rise** crisis card appears. Files against the King—bribe trails, written orders, blackmail and bloody nights—raise pressure from the courts. Past a threshold, the **The Court Considers Stripping Your Powers** card appears. You will see a STREET or COURT warning in the ticker beforehand. Ignore either crisis for a week and you can fall to an uprising or be stripped of power.
8. **Power centres do not wait:** Each week, people make small moves in their own sphere: Arden appoints officers, Brandt rallies the provinces, Kessler finds room in the budget. You will see them as “MOVE: …” in the ticker and with portraits under **Since last week** on the Agenda. Watching their moves is the best way to spot who is gaining ground.
9. **Old decisions return:** A choice you make today may return as an event weeks later. Each of the nine power centres has a story of rising, being tested, and staying loyal or breaking away. Your choices shape its final scene.
10. **Your rule gets a name:** Around week 60, the Palace starts calling your orders and alliances a regime. You will see this in the news and under **Since last week**. In the final stretch, a power centre suited to that style may call you to account. The label does not reveal hidden power values.

## Actions

| Action | What it does | Cost or risk |
|---|---|---|
| Summon to Court | Calms someone a little. Plotters often evade your questions; rumours about the innocent may fade. | Leaves no trace; once per person every 4 weeks |
| Promote | Grants power and gratitude. | They grow stronger and more ambitious; you owe them |
| Secret Payment | Buys loyalty. | 5 wealth; fades over time and leaves a trace |
| Verify Report | Cross-checks one report against other sources. | The result is noisy |
| Dismiss | Removes someone from play. | Anyone who knows their secrets may strike back |
| Investigate | Uncovers a weakness, then a breaking point. | May fail |
| Blackmail | Binds someone through fear using a known weakness. | Leaves a trace and may backfire |
| Written / Verbal Order | Suppresses unrest. | A written order leaves evidence |
| Hint | A deniable order. | An ambitious aide may overreach |
| Investigation | Asks a powerful figure for leads on people of interest. | Leads may be wrong |
| Covert Operation | Use 1–3 people to distract, divide the opposition, or weaken a rival. | 2 actions; a larger circle is more likely to leak |
| Set Them Against Each Other | Select exactly two people; if they feud, both lose power and ambition, weakening plot coordination. | 2 actions; failure exposes you and creates a secret; wait 6 weeks before targeting the same pair |
| Promise | Pledge the Regency, a contract, or immunity. | Keep it when due or pay the price |

In the development build, actions are grouped into seven tabs: **GAIN · PROMISE · LEARN · ORDER · PRESSURE · OPERATION · STATE**. Select a tab to see its actions. Hover over an action to read what it does; after an action, its result appears below the panel. To select several people for an operation, turn on **Multi-select** under OPERATION, or Ctrl-click.

## How the state works (development build)

The Capital map's Ministry of Finance, Court, General Staff and Palace panels describe institutions as **strained / functioning / sound**. A leader may make things sound better than they are. Compare their report with **Verify** to get a more reliable band for a while. Follow the treasury through the Finance panel; it is not the same as your personal wealth.

When the quarterly budget arrives, choose austerity, a balanced budget, generosity or a Palace share. If you postpone, the balanced budget takes effect. Under STATE, **Appoint Loyalist** binds the leader to you but makes the institution less effective; **Appoint Meritocrat** improves the institution but gives its leader more independence. You cannot appoint to the same institution in consecutive weeks. Read institution panels, leaders' reports and ticker warnings to see the effects.

**Award Contract to Halden** increases your personal wealth but drains the state treasury and weakens the Ministry of Finance; its commission leaves a trace in Files. As Halden's holding grows, so does its media influence. Read the **Influence** and **Media** bands on the person card: a friendly paper may one day turn on the Palace. On Halden's demand card, you can give him a share, refuse, set up a rival paper with Leyla, or ask Falk for help. Every route has a political cost; postponing the demand is a choice too.

### The heir and foreign relations

**Prepare Heir** under STATE gradually raises Julian's readiness. His card shows only **Childish**, **Developing** or **Ready**; promoting him too aggressively may unsettle ambitious people at court.

Elena Varos describes the three foreign powers as **Cold / Distant / Close**. The Asteria, Drakon and Seravia missions on the Capital map show the same information. Use **Verify** to check Elena's optimistic account against the records for a short time. **Send Delegation** under STATE improves one relationship slightly at the expense of the other two. At the end of the reign, the Truth File records these ties and the heir's condition.

## Secrets and common knowledge

Your covert actions leave traces in the **Files** tab. The more people know a secret, the faster it spreads: first as **rumour**, then as **public knowledge**. A weakness everyone knows can help your enemies unite and bring the courts closer to the Palace. The public eventually forgets old scandals, but several breaking at once can shake the throne. You can **suppress** or **destroy** evidence, or **leak** evidence about your rivals.

## How you can lose

- A **coup** led by the army or the dynasty
- A **popular uprising**
- The Assembly and courts **strip you of power** after scandals accumulate
- The throne's support collapses completely

## The end of a reign

Your reign ends after 104 weeks or when you fall. You receive a **title**. If you survive, it often reflects your style of rule; if your institutions were exceptionally weak or strong, you may instead become the **King of a Ruined State** or the **Merit King**. A fallen ruler is remembered as the **Overthrown King**. Your summary describes your regime, its final threat, and the condition of the state.

The ending is divided into tabs:

- **Summary:** How it ended, the length of your reign, your promise record, new achievements, and your latest decisions.
- **Chart:** What really happened—the hidden course of the game, institutional performance, and Sarne's share of power.
- **Historians:** The court historian and the exiled historian tell two different versions of your reign.
- **Truth File:** Who lied to you, and when. Below it are the **Assembly Record** (how often each adviser made a recommendation, how often you followed it, and how often it misled you), the **Plot Ledger** (who grew uneasy, joined the conspiracy, or withdrew, and when), and the **State Ledger** (appointments, the weakest week, contracts, and hostile coverage).
- **Decisions:** Every decision you made or deferred, newest first.

Your reign is added to the **Royal Archive**. Any new achievements are announced; all 22 achievements are listed there.

## Shortcuts

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| Space | End the week |
| Esc | Open/close the menu, close a window, or skip a cinematic |

The game saves automatically at the end of each week. When a reign ends, its save is cleared; history moves in one direction.

## Settings

The main menu has four buttons: **Continue** (or **Return to Game** during a reign), **New Game**, **Settings**, and **Quit**. Links below them open How to Play, the Royal Archive, and About.

Choose **Easy**, **Normal**, or **Hard** on the New Game page. Settings include music and effects volume, full screen, shorter transitions, adviser tips, the opening cinematic, starting each week at the desk, and language. Press Esc to return from a submenu to the main menu.

## Tips

- Read the Palace Secretary's notes during the first few weeks.
- Never let one person become strong enough to overthrow you alone. If someone rises, strengthen a rival.
- When reports conflict, use **Verify**. Over time, the source ledger will show who tends to embellish.
- You can promise the same office to two people. Sooner or later, they will find out.
